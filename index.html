Public groups are open to strangers. Never send money, documents or your home address to anyone in a group, and be careful who you travel with.

Travel safety tips

  • Travel in daylight. Leave early so you reach camp before dark.
  • Board only at a busy public park. Never meet at a private or isolated place.
  • Never accept a ride in a private car offered by a stranger.
  • Don't pay the full fare to an unknown individual before you travel.
  • Only post transport you have arranged yourself. This site does not list drivers or vouch for anyone.
  • Share your live location on WhatsApp for the whole trip.
  • Keep your call-up letter and money in different bags. Keep valuables close.

🎒 Camp checklist

Tap to tick. Your progress is saved on this phone.

This is a general list. Your call-up letter and the official NYSC portal are the final word on what to bring.

Registration steps (general)

  1. Arrive at camp with your printed call-up letter and original documents.
  2. Go through document screening at the registration hall.
  3. Complete the medical checks and biometrics.
  4. Collect your kit and accommodation slot, and get your platoon.

🏕️ Camp life

What a normal day looks like

Early wake-up (bugle), morning parade and drills, physical training, lectures, skills training (SAED), then sports, cultural and social activities. Exact times differ by camp. Sundays are often lighter, with fellowship and rest.

Your first 3 days
  • Registration: document screening, medical check, then your platoon and accommodation.
  • Swearing-in is on Friday, 2 October.
  • Keep your call-up letter and photocopies with you.
Camp market (Mammy market)

Food, snacks, drinks, toiletries and small items are sold inside camp. Prices differ by camp, so ask around and agree the price before you buy. Carry small cash and don't show large sums.

Rules to respect
  • Don't leave camp without permission. If you must, ask your platoon officer for an exeat (permission to leave).
  • No vehicles or motorbikes inside camp.
  • No drugs. Being rude to camp officials can bring punishment.
  • Wear the right kit for each activity, usually white kit for parade and drills.
  • Follow instructions from camp officials. Discipline is strict.
Health and safety in camp
  • Drink water, wash your hands and eat properly.
  • Report illness early at the camp clinic.
  • Padlock your box. Keep your phone, ATM card and documents secure.
  • Never give your ATM PIN, OTP or bank details to anyone.
  • Be careful with strangers offering "help", quick money or favours.
  • Report theft or harassment to your platoon officer or camp officials.
Skills training (SAED)

NYSC runs Skills Acquisition and Entrepreneurship Development training during camp. Pick a skill you would really use after service, and take the sessions seriously.

Phone, money and visitors
  • Bring a power bank; charging points can be crowded.
  • Bring enough cash. Banks and ATMs may be far or limited.
  • Visitors are usually met at a designated visitors' area, not in the hostels.
Camp rules can differ by state. Follow your camp officials and NYSC's official channels.

➡️ After camp: your service year

Posting day and your PPA
  • At the end of camp you are posted to a PPA (Place of Primary Assignment): a school, hospital, government office, company or organization.
  • Report to your PPA promptly and complete the documentation. Keep your call-up letter, posting letter and photocopies together.
  • If your PPA rejects you, take their rejection to your LGI. NYSC will help you get another PPA.
Register with your LGI

The LGI (Local Government Inspector) is your NYSC contact in your local government. Register with them as soon as you arrive. They help with PPA problems, clearance and welfare issues.

CDS (Community Development Service)
  • Every corper joins a CDS group and attends one weekday a week. It is often Thursday, so confirm with your LGI.
  • Attendance is compulsory. Missing too many days can delay your clearance and allowance.
  • Dues and fines differ by group, so ask the executives what they are before you pay.
Monthly clearance and allowance
  • Each month your PPA gives you a clearance form to sign. You need it to receive your allowance, so complete it early.
  • The federal allowance is ₦77,000 a month. It is usually paid late in the month, and your first payment after camp can come later.
  • Some states and PPAs add extra pay. Ask your LGI what applies to you.
  • Make sure your bank details are correct, so payment isn't delayed.
Travelling during your service year

Do not travel outside your state without permission. You need approval from your PPA and from the state NYSC office. Avoid night travel, and use the Safety tab before any trip.

Changing your posting (redeployment)

Corpers can apply for redeployment for reasons like health (with medical proof), marriage, or security concerns. You can apply during camp or soon after it. NYSC reviews each request, so don't assume it will be approved. Ask your camp officials about the process and deadlines.

Find a room near your PPA

Use the Housing tab to find a roommate or a place to share, and read the "Before you pay for a house" warning first.

Keep your records

Keep copies of your posting letter, PPA acceptance letter, clearance forms and CDS attendance. You'll need them when you complete service.

Processes can differ by state and PPA. Your LGI and your state NYSC office have the final say.

🛑 Before you pay for a house

  • Inspect in person, in daylight, with someone. Never pay for a place you have not seen.
  • Don't send money to someone you haven't met. Fake landlords and agents target new corpers.
  • Be careful with rushed payments, "many people are interested" and rent that is too cheap.
  • Ask for a receipt or written agreement, and confirm the person really owns or manages the place.
  • Meet a possible roommate in a public place first. Send the address and their number to someone you trust.
  • Ask other corpers in your state group which areas are safe.

🚨 Scams targeting corpers

Golden rule: NYSC has said deployment, posting, relocation, and camp services like accommodation and feeding are free. Nobody can sell you a posting or a redeployment.

"Pay for posting or redeployment"

Fake agents and fake "NYSC" accounts offer concessional posting, redeployment or age reduction for money. NYSC says requests are treated on merit and never for a fee. Don't pay anyone.

Fake links, websites and pages

Scammers send links asking you to log in to "check your deployment". Use only the official NYSC portal and verified NYSC accounts. Never type your login details on a link sent to you.

Fake notices and letters

Fake "URGENT NOTICE" messages on fake letterheads circulate every batch. Before you act on any notice, confirm it with your camp officials, your state NYSC office or your LGI.

Fake medical reports

Buying a medical certificate or faking a report is fraud and can land you in trouble. Get a fitness certificate only from a recognised public health facility.

"NYSC kit" sellers

NYSC has said kit items issued in camp are free. Be careful paying strangers who sell "official" kits or camp "slots".

Bank, OTP and ATM tricks

No one from NYSC, your bank or a "posting office" needs your ATM PIN, OTP or BVN. If you already paid a scammer, tell your bank immediately and report to the police.

Fake rooms and transport

Read "Before you pay for a house" in the Housing tab and "Before you board" in the Rides tab.
